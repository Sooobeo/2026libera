L193:                                  THE EMPLOYMENT SITUATION - JUNE 2026
L194: 
L195: 
L196:     Both total nonfarm payroll employment (+57,000) and the unemployment rate (4.2 percent)
L197:     changed little in June, the U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics reported today. Employment
L198:     continued to trend up in professional and business services, social assistance, and health
L199:     care. Leisure and hospitality lost jobs.
L200: 
L201:     This news release presents statistics from two monthly surveys. The household survey measures
L202:     labor force status, including unemployment, by demographic characteristics. The establishment
L203:     survey measures nonfarm employment, hours, and earnings by industry. For more information
L204:     about the concepts and statistical methodology used in these two surveys, see the Technical
L205:     Note.
L206: 
L207:     Household Survey Data
L208: 
L209:     Both the unemployment rate, at 4.2 percent, and the number of unemployed people, at 7.1
L210:     million, changed little in June. These measures also changed little over the year.
L211:     (See table A-1.)
L212: 
L213:     Among the major worker groups, the unemployment rates showed little or no change in June for
L214:     adult men (3.9 percent), adult women (3.7 percent), teenagers (14.6 percent), and people who
L215:     are White (3.6 percent), Black (6.6 percent), Asian (3.9 percent), or Hispanic (5.2 percent).
L216:     (See tables A-1, A-2, and A-3.)
L217:     The number of long-term unemployed (those jobless for 27 weeks or more) changed little at 1.9
L218:     million in June but is up by 286,000 over the year. The long-term unemployed accounted for
L219:     27.3 percent of all unemployed people in June. (See table A-12.)
L220: 
L221:     The labor force participation rate decreased by 0.3 percentage point to 61.5 percent in June,
L222:     and the employment-population ratio edged down by 0.2 percentage point to 59.0 percent. Both
L223:     measures changed little over the year after accounting for annual population control
L224:     adjustments. (See table A-1.)
L225: 
L226:     The number of people employed part time for economic reasons changed little at 4.7 million in
L227:     June. These individuals would have preferred full-time employment but were working part time
L228:     because their hours had been reduced or they were unable to find full-time jobs.
L229:     (See table A-8.)
L230:     In June, the number of people not in the labor force who currently want a job changed little
L231:     at 6.0 million. These individuals were not counted as unemployed because they were not
L232:     actively looking for work during the 4 weeks preceding the survey or were unavailable to take
L233:     a job. (See table A-1.)
L234: 
L235:     Among those not in the labor force who wanted a job, the number of people marginally attached
L236:     to the labor force changed little at 1.8 million in June. These individuals wanted and were
L237:     available for work and had looked for a job sometime in the prior 12 months but had not looked
L238:     for work in the 4 weeks preceding the survey. The number of discouraged workers, a subset of
L239:     the marginally attached who believed that no jobs were available for them, was essentially
L240:     unchanged in June at 477,000. (See Summary table A.)
L241: 
L242:     Establishment Survey Data
L243:     Total nonfarm payroll employment changed little in June (+57,000), roughly in line with the
L244:     average monthly change over the prior 12 months (+36,000). In June, employment continued to
L245:     trend up in professional and business services, social assistance, and health care. Employment
L246:     in leisure and hospitality declined. (See table B-1.)
L247: 
L248:     Employment in professional and business services continued to trend up in June (+36,000). The
L249:     industry has added 172,000 jobs since a recent low in October 2025.
L250: 
L251:     Social assistance added 25,000 jobs in June, primarily in individual and family services
L252:     (+17,000). Over the prior 12 months, social assistance had added an average of 16,000 jobs per
L253:     month.
L254: 
L255:     In June, employment in health care continued its upward trend (+22,000) but at a slower pace
L256:     than the average monthly gain over the prior 12 months (+38,000). In June, hospitals added
L257:     9,000 jobs.
L258:     Leisure and hospitality employment declined by 61,000 in June, reflecting weaker than usual
L259:     seasonal hiring. Thus far in 2026, employment in the industry has shown little net change.
L260: 
L261:     Employment showed little or no change over the month in other major industries, including
L262:     mining, quarrying, and oil and gas extraction; construction; manufacturing; wholesale trade;
L263:     retail trade; transportation and warehousing; information; financial activities; other
L264:     services; and government.
L265: 
L266:     In June, average hourly earnings for all employees on private nonfarm payrolls rose by 13
L267:     cents, or 0.3 percent, to $37.64. Over the year, average hourly earnings have increased by 3.5
L268:     percent. In June, average hourly earnings of private-sector production and nonsupervisory
L269:     employees rose by 7 cents, or 0.2 percent, to $32.38. (See tables B-3 and B-8.)
L270: 
L271:     The average workweek for all employees on private nonfarm payrolls was unchanged at 34.3 hours
L272:     in June. In manufacturing, the average workweek edged down to 40.3 hours, and overtime edged
L273:     up to 3.2 hours. The average workweek for production and nonsupervisory employees on private
L274:     nonfarm payrolls declined by 0.1 hour to 33.7 hours. (See tables B-2 and B-7.)
L275: 
L276:     The change in total nonfarm payroll employment for April was revised down by 31,000, from
L277:     +179,000 to +148,000, and the change for May was revised down by 43,000, from +172,000 to
L278:     +129,000. With these revisions, employment in April and May combined is 74,000 lower than
L279:     previously reported. (Monthly revisions result from additional reports received from
L280:     businesses and government agencies since the last published estimates and from the
L281:     recalculation of seasonal factors.)
